Your data

Privacy

Ixel has no account, no server of its own, and nothing that reports back to us. It runs on your computer and talks only to the services you set up, when you use them. This page lists every one, and everything it keeps.

In short

  • No telemetry. No analytics, no crash reports, no usage pings. We don't know who uses Ixel or how.
  • No Ixel account, and no Ixel server. Your questions go from your computer to the AI companies you chose, and nowhere else.
  • Your keys stay yours. Each key goes only to its own company, and never over plain http:// except to your own computer (a git host's token also across your Tailscale network, which encrypts it).
  • Private keeps it at home. Turn on Private in Settings, and only the models on your own computers answer: your questions and their answers stay on your computer or your network, as long as your servers run the models themselves, and every other model sits out.
  • Some companies train on what you send. It depends on the company and how you connect, usually an API key or a sign-in plan. Which ones, and how to keep yours out.
  • Some things are kept on your computer, listed below with how to clear them.
The companies you use

What you send to Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, xAI or any other model company is covered by that company's terms, not ours. Ixel can't make them forget it. Which companies may train on it says what each one does. For questions that must not leave your computer, use a local model and turn on Private.

Which companies may train on it

Once your question reaches a company, Ixel can't keep it private. That company's rules decide whether it keeps what you send, uses it to train its models, or lets its staff read it. Mostly it comes down to how you connect: API keys on a paid account usually aren't trained on, while sign-in plans and free tiers often are.

Our part, and theirs

Ixel sends a question only where you tell it to and keeps no copy on any server. What a company does after that is set by its terms, which you agreed to with that company. We can't change them, so we tell you what they say, and the choice is yours.

Two tools that Ixel doesn't use, since people ask:

  • Google Antigravity. Google keeps your prompts, code and replies to improve its products and models, and its employees and contractors may read them. You can opt out in Antigravity's Settings, but its terms don't say whether that also stops people reading them. Its FAQ says reaching Antigravity through other tools breaks its terms and can cost you the account, and points those tools to a Gemini API key, which is what Ixel uses.
  • Cline. Its free models may use what you send to improve the model, and most come from labs it doesn't name. On Cline's own provider, Cline sees your prompts and code and keeps them for a time its privacy policy (Sep 24, 2025) doesn't state. Your own API key or a local model avoids both.

Keep your questions out of training

  1. Use an API key on a paid account

    Anthropic, OpenAI and xAI don't train on API use by default, and Google doesn't once billing is on for the key's Cloud project. A free Gemini key is trained on and read by reviewers, except in the EEA, Switzerland and the UK.

  2. On a plan, turn training off

    For Claude Code, the model improvement setting in Claude's Privacy settings. For Codex, Improve the model for everyone in ChatGPT's Data controls, and Include environments in Codex's settings. For Copilot, Allow GitHub to use my data for AI model training in your Copilot settings. Each one covers what you send from then on.

  3. Skip the ones that train on every plan

    Such as Kimi and DeepSeek, unless you've opted out with them. Free models in OpenCode Zen and Cline may use what you send, too.

  4. Keep it on your computer

    For anything that mustn't leave it, use a local model and turn on Private. No company sees the question at all.

Not training on something isn't the same as not keeping it: most companies keep what you send for up to 30 days to catch abuse, and longer if it's flagged.

What Ixel strips or refuses

  • Photo details are removed. Every picture you attach is drawn again before it's sent, which leaves out what a camera writes into a photo, such as where it was taken.
  • Secrets in code are refused. Code or files that look like they hold a key or a token aren't sent, and Ixel says where they are. Handoff refuses them on the board too.
  • Subscription programs don't get your keys. The keys Ixel keeps aren't passed to Claude Code, Codex and the others, so your subscription is what gets used.
  • No redirects. A key is sent only to the address you set, never on to wherever that address points.
  • A folder can't change Ixel's settings. Ixel ignores settings files in the folder you run it in, so a project you download can't point your keys elsewhere.

Honest limits

  • Keys are kept in plain text, in a file only you can read, not in your system's keychain.
  • On Windows and Linux, the app window is Edge or Chrome. Its own background services follow Microsoft's or Google's settings, not Ixel's.
  • A question typed on the command line (ixel review "…") is saved in your shell's history, like any command. Type it in the ixel chat to avoid that.
  • The app's window can keep its conversations. The page holds an open window's conversations in its session storage. Ixel never saves them, but the window's browser may write that storage to its profile folder, where it can stay after you close the window. Clear it if that matters to you.
  • Private covers your questions, not everything. Pictures you make with ixel image still go to xAI or OpenAI, the Board's Review… and Fix… send work to the agent you pick, and a question asked through the plugin came from that app's model in the first place. Opening Settings and checking your models still send your keys to their companies, with no question. An ixel chat already open when you turn Private on doesn't follow it until you start it again.
  • Private trusts the servers you add. It can't see what a server on your network does with a question, so a server that passes questions on to a company still counts as yours. Private has the details.
  • Deleted board text can linger in the database file's free space until the database is compacted. Deleting the .handoff folder removes it all.

Leave as little as possible

  1. Use local models, with Private on

    For anything that mustn't leave your computer, add a local model and turn on Private in Settings, under Asking. Then only your own models answer, and nothing is sent if none of them can.

  2. Ask in the chat or the app

    Rather than with ixel review "…", which keeps your last three questions in conversation.json and puts the question in your shell's history. Ixel doesn't write the chat's conversations anywhere, or the app's to a file of its own.

  3. Clear what's kept

    Windows

    Remove-Item "$HOME\.config\ixel-mat\conversation.json", "$HOME\.config\ixel-mat\machines.log*" -ErrorAction SilentlyContinue

    macOS

    rm -f ~/.config/ixel-mat/conversation.json ~/.config/ixel-mat/machines.log*

    Linux

    rm -f ~/.config/ixel-mat/conversation.json ~/.config/ixel-mat/machines.log*

    With Ixel closed, delete the window's browser storage too: %LOCALAPPDATA%\IxelMAT\window on Windows, ~/.local/state/ixel-mat/window on Linux, and ~/Library/WebKit/com.ixelai.ixel (and ~/Library/Application Support/IxelMAT/window, if Ixel ever used Chrome there) on a Mac.

    For a project's board, handoff delete T-N removes one task, or delete the project's .handoff folder to remove them all.

  4. Turn off the update check

    Add this to config.toml, and run ixel update yourself now and then:

    [updates]
check = false

This website

ixelai.com has no cookies, no analytics, no tracking, no forms and no accounts. Its fonts, scripts and pictures are served from the site itself, and each page's security policy stops it from connecting anywhere else. These docs read your browser's platform, in the page only, to show your system's steps first; nothing is stored or sent.
