Your data

Privacy

Ixel has no account, no server of its own, and nothing that reports back to us. It runs on your computer and talks only to the services you set up, when you use them. This page lists every one, and everything it keeps.

In short

  • No telemetry. No analytics, no crash reports, no usage pings. We don't know who uses Ixel or how.
  • No Ixel account, and no Ixel server. Your questions go from your computer to the AI companies you chose, and nowhere else.
  • Your keys stay yours. Each key goes only to its own company, and never over plain http:// except to your own computer (a git host's token also across your Tailscale network, which encrypts it). On your computer they're encrypted, with the key that opens them in your system's keychain. On a computer with no keychain Ixel can use (a Linux server, say), they're in a plain-text file only you can read, and Health says so.
  • Private keeps it at home. Turn on Private in Settings, and only the models on your own computers answer: your questions and their answers stay on your computer or your network, as long as your servers run the models themselves, and every other model sits out.
  • Some companies train on what you send. It depends on the company and how you connect, usually an API key or a sign-in plan. Which ones, and how to keep yours out.
  • Little is kept, and not for long. Your last ixel review questions for a day, a log of what Machines did for 30 days (never a command's text), and nothing of the app's conversations once Ixel stops. ixel forget clears it all. The full list.
The companies you use

What you send to Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, xAI or any other model company is covered by that company's terms, not ours. Ixel can't make them forget it. Which companies may train on it says what each one does. For questions that must not leave your computer, use a local model and turn on Private.

Which companies may train on it

Once your question reaches a company, Ixel can't keep it private. That company's rules decide whether it keeps what you send, uses it to train its models, or lets its staff read it. Mostly it comes down to how you connect: API keys on a paid account usually aren't trained on, while sign-in plans and free tiers often are.

Our part, and theirs

Ixel sends a question only where you tell it to and keeps no copy on any server. What a company does after that is set by its terms, which you agreed to with that company. We can't change them, so we tell you what they say, and the choice is yours.

Keep your questions out of training

  1. Use an API key on a paid account

    Anthropic, OpenAI and xAI don't train on API use by default, and Google doesn't once billing is on for the key's Cloud project. A free Gemini key is trained on and may be read by reviewers, except in the EEA, Switzerland and the UK.

  2. On a plan, turn training off

    For Claude Code, the training choice in Claude's Privacy settings. For Codex, Improve the model for everyone in ChatGPT's Data controls, and Include environments in Codex's settings. For Copilot, Allow GitHub to use my data for AI model training in your Copilot settings. For Grok Build, leave Coding data, retention, and training (/privacy in grok) off, and turn off Improve the model in Grok's Data Controls.

  3. Skip the ones that train with no switch to stop it

    Such as Kimi and DeepSeek, unless you've asked them to stop by email or support. Free models in OpenCode Zen and Cline may use what you send, too.

  4. Keep it on your computer

    For anything that mustn't leave it, use a local model and turn on Private. Then only the models on your own computers answer; Honest limits says what Private doesn't cover.

Not training on something isn't the same as not keeping it: several of these companies keep what you send for up to 30 days to catch abuse, and longer if it's flagged.

What the AI programs keep

A program on your computer that Ixel hands a question to may save it in its own folders. Claude Code and Codex are told not to. Gemini CLI, Copilot and OpenCode have no such switch, so once each run is over, Ixel removes what that run saved, and nothing else: never a login, a setting, or a session of yours. Grok Build gets a home folder of its own for each run, holding only a copy of your login, and Ixel deletes it afterwards. We checked this with Gemini CLI 0.62, Copilot 1.0.91, OpenCode 1.18 and 2.0, and Grok Build 1.0.46.

What Ixel strips or refuses

  • Photo details are removed. Every picture you attach is drawn again before it's sent, which leaves out what a camera writes into a photo, such as where it was taken.
  • Secrets in code are refused. Code or files that look like they hold a key or a token aren't sent, and Ixel says where they are. Handoff refuses them on the board too.
  • Subscription programs don't get your keys. The keys Ixel keeps aren't passed to Claude Code, Codex and the others, so your subscription is what gets used. The one exception is Gemini CLI: it gets your Google (Gemini) key while it isn't signed in another way.
  • No redirects. A key is sent only to the address you set, never on to wherever that address points.
  • A folder can't change Ixel's settings. Ixel ignores settings files in the folder you run it in, so a project you download can't point your keys elsewhere.

Honest limits

  • Your keychain gives your keys to programs you run. The key that opens your saved keys is in your keychain, and on Windows any program you run can ask Credential Manager for it, as can any program in your session once a Linux keyring is unlocked. A Mac asks you first. A copy of your home folder is only as safe as your keychain's password, and a Linux keyring with a blank password protects nothing. On a computer with no keychain Ixel can use, your keys are in plain text, in a file only you can read.
  • On Windows and Linux, the app window is Edge or Chrome. Ixel turns off its background services, but two requests to Google are left (above), and Edge's own Microsoft services follow Microsoft's settings, not Ixel's.
  • A question typed on the command line (ixel review "…") is saved in your shell's history, like any command. Type it in the ixel chat to avoid that.
  • Private covers your questions, not everything. Pictures you make with ixel image still go to xAI or OpenAI, the Board's Review… and Fix… send work to the agent you pick, and a question asked through the plugin came from that app's model in the first place. Opening Settings and checking your models still send your keys to their companies, with no question. An ixel chat already open when you turn Private on doesn't follow it until you start it again.
  • Private trusts the servers you add. It can't see what a server on your network does with a question, so a server that passes questions on to a company still counts as yours. Private has the details.
  • Your own command-line agents. One you add yourself gets the question on stdin, unless its prompt_via says "flag", "arg" or "auto": then it's on the program's command line, where other programs on the computer can read it.
  • Deleting isn't shredding. Ixel and Handoff overwrite deleted text inside their own files, but a disk can keep old copies of a file it rewrote (most SSDs do), and so do backups and synced folders.

Leave as little as possible

  1. Use local models, with Private on

    For anything that mustn't leave your computer, add a local model and turn on Private in Settings, under Asking. Then only your own models answer, and nothing is sent if none of them can.

  2. Ask in the chat or the app

    Rather than with ixel review "…", which keeps your last three questions in conversation.json for a day and puts the question in your shell's history. The chat and the app keep their conversations in memory only.

  3. Clear what's kept

    Close Ixel, then run:

    ixel forget

    It deletes conversation.json, the Machines log and the app window's browser storage, and says what it removed. In the app, Forget at the bottom of Settings clears the first two. Ixel's own Linux window keeps its storage in ~/.local/share/ixel and ~/.cache/ixel, which ixel forget names but doesn't delete: delete those folders too, with Ixel closed.

    For a project's board, handoff delete T-N removes one task with its results, and handoff clean removes finished tasks that haven't changed in 30 days (--older-than DAYS to change that), after listing them and asking. handoff keep 30 has the board do that by itself. Deleting the project's .handoff folder removes everything.

    If you uninstall Ixel, also remove the keychain item named Ixel (account keys): in Keychain Access on a Mac, Credential Manager on Windows (Windows Credentials), or your keyring app on Linux.

  4. Turn off the update check

    Add this to config.toml, and run ixel update yourself now and then:

    [updates]
check = false

This website

ixelai.com has no cookies, no analytics, no tracking, no forms and no accounts. Its fonts, scripts and pictures are served from the site itself, and each page's security policy stops it from connecting anywhere else. These docs read your browser's platform, in the page only, to show your system's steps first; nothing is stored or sent.
